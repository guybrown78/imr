There's no single legal interval. The law asks you to keep equipment safe, and the **IET Code of Practice for In-service Inspection and Testing of Electrical Equipment** gives suggested intervals based on the type of environment and how the item is used.

| Environment | Typical interval for a combined inspection and test |
| --- | --- |
| Construction sites (110V) | Every 3 months |
| Industrial units and workshops | Every 6 to 12 months |
| Hospitality kitchens and hire equipment | Every 6 to 12 months |
| Schools | Every 12 months (Class I) |
| Offices and shops | Every 24 to 48 months, with regular visual checks between tests |
| Rental properties | Usually at each change of tenancy, or yearly for HMOs |

## Why not just "every year"?
Annual testing is a sensible default for most businesses, and plenty of insurers ask for it. But an IT item that never moves from a desk is a lower risk than a kettle that's handled ten times a day, or a drill that's thrown in a van.

On every test we print a **recommended retest date on the tag**, based on how that item is actually used. So you test what needs it, and not what doesn't.

## What happens between tests
Get staff into the habit of a quick visual check. They should look for damaged cables, scorch marks, loose plugs and taped-up repairs. Anything suspicious should come out of use until it's been checked.
