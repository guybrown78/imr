---
title: Is PAT testing a legal requirement?
description: What the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989 actually say, and why a PAT certificate is still the easiest way to show you've kept equipment safe.
order: 3
---

**Short answer: the law doesn't use the words "PAT testing", but it does require you to keep electrical equipment safe, and testing with records is the accepted way to prove it.**

## What the law says
- **Electricity at Work Regulations 1989.** Employers must maintain electrical systems and equipment so they don't cause danger.
- **Health and Safety at Work Act 1974.** Employers owe a general duty of care to staff and visitors.
- **Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999.** You must assess risks, including electrical ones.
- **Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998.** Work equipment must be maintained in a safe condition.

None of these name a test or a frequency. They require the outcome, which is safe equipment. If something goes wrong, you'll need to show what you did to prevent it.

## Why most businesses test anyway
1. **Insurers** often ask for it, and may question a claim without it.
2. **Landlords, letting agents and HMO licences** commonly require a certificate.
3. **Event organisers, schools and contractors' clients** ask for it before you start work.
4. **It's proof.** A dated, itemised record shows an inspector exactly what was checked and when.

## Landlords
The 2020 private rented sector electrical regulations cover the fixed wiring (EICR), not appliances. But landlords must still make sure any appliances they supply are safe, and many HMO licences specifically ask for PAT records.

*This is general guidance, not legal advice. Check with your insurer or local authority if you're unsure what applies to you.*
