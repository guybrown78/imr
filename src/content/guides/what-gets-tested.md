---
title: What does PAT testing include?
description: Which appliances need PAT testing, what happens during a test, and what the pass/fail tag means.
order: 4
---

PAT testing covers anything that **plugs into a socket**. Hard-wired equipment is covered by fixed wiring inspection (EICR) instead.

## What we test
- **Class I** items (earthed, usually metal): kettles, toasters, fridges, PCs, heaters and washing machines
- **Class II** items (double insulated, marked with a square-in-a-square symbol): hairdryers, drills, lamps and most chargers
- **Extension leads and IEC power leads.** These count as separate items, and they're one of the most common failures.
- **110V site equipment** and transformers

## What happens on the day
1. **Formal visual inspection.** We check the plug, fuse rating, cable, cord grip and casing. Most faults are found here.
2. **Electrical tests.** Earth continuity (Class I), insulation resistance and, where suitable, a leakage or substitute leakage test.
3. **Tag and record.** Each item gets a tag showing the test date, the result and when it's next due, and it's added to your test record.
4. **Certificate.** You get a certificate and a record of every item tested.

## If something fails
We label it **FAIL**, take it out of use and tell you why. Simple faults like a wrong fuse, a damaged plug or a split cord grip can usually be fixed there and then, with your OK, and retested.
